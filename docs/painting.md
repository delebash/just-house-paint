# Painting your house

Select a part on the **Parts** tab (or click it on the photo with **Select**), then pick a color on
the **Colors** tab. The paint shows at once. **No color** beside the chosen paint takes it off the
part; **Show more** under the colors loads the brand's next ones.

The **Parts** tab lists the parts to paint, each with its share of the house and its paint (**edited**
once you changed its outline by hand); the roof, windows, stone, brick and lamps are listed under
**Staying as they are**.

## How the color is shown

The app paints each part with the paint's exact color in the part's normal light, and keeps the
photo's own shadows, texture and lap lines: a wall in shade is the same paint, darker. Beside each
painted part the **Parts** tab says how close the painted wall comes to the paint chip — or, with
**On it now** set, to the chip in that part's light — **ΔE** under 1 is a difference most people
can't see, under 2 is a close match (its tag turns green).

**On this wall** — under the chosen paint on the **Colors** tab, the same paint as it shows on the
selected part: **in shade**, on **most of it** and **in light**. The light one is the chip — in the
part's own light when **On it now** is set, and through **Light**; a wall in the shade of a roof or a
tree shows mostly the darker ones, as real paint would.

**On it now** — if you know the paint a part has today, tell the app: on the **Colors** tab switch
**The new color** to **On it now** and click that paint (search for it, or type its color under the
list). It doesn't paint anything: the app compares that paint with how your camera photographed it
on the part's normally lit areas, which gives the photo's real light there — how bright, and its tint
(shade and an overcast sky are bluish). Every new color on that part then shows in that light, as
your camera would have taken it, and so does the **AI photo**. The line **On it now: …** shows the
paint; **Clear** forgets it.
It is kept with the house. Not sure what's on it? Leave it: the paint then shows at its chip color
in the part's normal light.

**Light** — a brighter or duller day over the whole picture, photo and paint alike: 1 is the photo's own light.
The ΔE beside each part is always measured at the photo's own light.

**A dark finish painted light** — paint covers the old finish's own colors, not the boards' shadows.
When you paint a dark part (stained wood, a dark paint — a wall, a door, shutters, trim) much lighter,
the app takes out the old finish's blotches and grain and keeps the lines between the boards, as fresh
paint would look. It does this only for a dark finish, and only along the boards: stucco keeps its
texture; on brick the rows stay and the joints between bricks soften. Whether a finish is dark is read
from the photo, against its brightest parts, so a light part photographed in shade is painted as
photographed. In a photo taken against a bright sky, a light wall can read as dark: set its paint in
**On it now** and the app goes by that paint instead. The first light color on such a part, and the
first after you change its outline, take a moment longer.

**Texture** — how much of what's left of the photo's texture shows: lower it to calm it further; at 1
it is kept whole. The photo changes when you let go of the slider (or leave its box after typing a
number), not while you drag it.

## Comparing

**Compare with** puts something else on the left of a line across the photo: **The photo as taken**
(before and after), or one of the house's saved designs — so two designs sit side by side (open one,
compare with the other). The colors on screen stay on the right; the slider beside it moves the
line. **Side by side** shows the two as pictures next to each other instead — one zoom, one pan; the
brush, eraser, box and eyedropper work on the right one. **Nothing** takes the line away.

**Outlines** draws the edge of the **Selected part** (the default), of **All parts**, or none
(**Off**); the choice is kept. Zoom with the mouse wheel or the **+**, **−** and **Fit** buttons at
the photo's top right; drag with **Select** to move the photo.

## Undo and Reset

**Undo** and **Redo** (Ctrl+Z, and Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z) take back a color, or a change to a part's outline made
with the brush, the eraser or a box, and put it back again. They remember the last 50 steps while
the house is open; **Find the parts again** starts them over. Undo also puts back which design was
open. The keys work anywhere on the page except while you type in a field (there they undo your
typing) or while a dialog is open.

**Reset** offers **Remove all colors** — the photo as taken — and, when you changed the colors of
an open design, **Back to** that design's saved colors. Undo brings back what a Reset took away.

## Designs

Save the colors on the house as a **design** on the **Designs** tab: **Save as a new design**. A
house keeps several; click one to put its colors back on. Once you change a color, the open design
shows **changed** and **Save** keeps the change. Opening another design, or leaving the house, while
there are unsaved changes asks first. Each design's buttons rename it and delete it (its AI photos
stay). A house keeps up to 30 designs (**App Settings → General → Designs and paint files**).

A design saved before the house had some of its trim parts (soffits and fascia, window trim,
decorations) leaves them unpainted. Opening it says which parts have no color, and **Paint them the trim's color** paints
them the design's trim color — nothing changes until you press it, and **Undo** takes it back.

## With the keyboard

**Tab** goes through the buttons, lists and fields; whatever has the keyboard's focus has a ring
around it.

- **A part** — on the **Parts** tab, Tab to it and press **Enter**.
- **A paint** — the colors on the **Colors** tab are one Tab stop (the chosen one, or the first): the
  arrow keys move between them, up and down a row too, **Home** and **End** go to the first and the
  last, and **Enter** or **Space** picks one. Tab goes on to **Show more** and what follows.
- **A color of your own** — type its hex under **An exact color**; a part's color as photographed is
  the small round swatch on its row on the **Parts** tab.
- **The photo** — Tab reaches it too; once you have zoomed in with **+**, the arrow keys move it.
- **Drawing** — the **Brush**, the **Eraser**, the **Box** and **Pick a color** need a mouse, a pen or
  touch.

## Saving the picture

**Save image** writes the painted photo at full size — up to 16 megapixels: a bigger photo is saved
scaled down to that, the same shape (**App Settings → General → Photos** → **Save image and the AI
photo, at most (megapixels)** sets it). A big photo takes a few seconds; the button waits meanwhile,
and the rest of the app keeps working. **Copy the color list** gives you the
parts and their paints (brand number and name) to take to the store. **Save a color card** on the
**Designs** tab makes one picture of both: the painted photo, then each painted part with its
paint's swatch, brand number, name and hex.

> Colors on a screen are never exactly the paint — your screen, the photo's light and the paint's
> sheen all differ. Check a real sample on your house before you buy.
